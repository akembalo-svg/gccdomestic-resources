# 🇮🇩 Indonesian Domestic Workers in the Gulf

Indonesia remains a key source of skilled domestic workers for Gulf households, particularly valued by Muslim families for shared religious practice and Halal cooking expertise.

## Why Gulf families choose Indonesian workers

- ☪️ **Muslim-majority** — observes prayer times, fasts during Ramadan naturally
- 🍳 **Halal-by-default cooking** — no need to explain dietary restrictions
- 👶 **Childcare-friendly** — patient with children, often experienced with infants
- 🌏 **Cultural adaptability** — Indonesian culture shares hospitality norms with Gulf cultures

## Indonesian hiring process

1. **BP2MI** (Badan Pelindungan Pekerja Migran Indonesia) — Indonesia's protection agency for migrant workers, formerly BNP2TKI
2. **Pre-employment training** — 200-hour course covering language, household skills, and cultural orientation
3. **PMI ID (Indonesian Migrant Worker Card)** required for all departing workers
4. **Source agency** (PPTKIS-licensed) pairs with Gulf agency
5. **Worker arrives via Jakarta** or other approved airports
6. **Embassy registration** in destination country

Typical timeline: **6-10 weeks** for UAE/Qatar/Bahrain/Oman, **8-14 weeks** for Saudi/Kuwait due to higher demand volume.

## Post-2015 Saudi-Indonesia moratorium context

Indonesia's 2015 moratorium on sending domestic workers to KSA was partially lifted in 2018 under the "SPSK" (Saudi Special Placement) framework. Indonesian workers headed to Saudi must now go through this stricter program, which includes:

- Higher minimum wage (SAR 1,500/month for housemaids)
- Mandatory accident insurance
- 24-hour emergency hotline access
- Restricted to Riyadh, Jeddah, Madinah, and Makkah employers initially

For UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, and Kuwait — Indonesian recruitment continues normally.
